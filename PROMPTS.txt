THE SUPPLIER BILL CHECKER — the exact prompts from the episode, word for word.
Paste them into your AI assistant in order. Prompt 1 builds the first version; prompts 2 and 3
are the fixes from the episode — they exist because the first two versions were wrong in ways
worth understanding before you let any tool argue with your suppliers for you.

================================ PROMPT 1 ================================

Build me a single HTML file I can open in my browser, no internet, no account, that checks my
supplier bills for me.

What it's for: I run a two-bay auto repair shop. I buy parts and supplies from a handful of
suppliers at prices we agreed on, and every month a stack of invoices shows up. I don't have
time to check them line by line, so I mostly just pay them.

Data in: two pastes.
1) My agreed price list: CSV with columns Supplier, Item Code, Description, Unit, Agreed Price —
   what each supplier agreed to charge me per unit.
2) The month's bills: CSV with columns Invoice, Date, Supplier, Item Code, Description, Qty,
   Unit Price, Line Total — every line of every supplier invoice.

What I want out: tell me if anything on these bills is wrong — wrong prices, bad math, anything
that doesn't look right — and what it adds up to, so I know whether checking bills is even worth
my time.

================================ PROMPT 2 ================================

The first version missed almost everything and flagged the wrong things. Rebuild the checking
rules, and bring in the third file this job actually needs:

3) My receiving log: CSV with columns Date, Supplier, Item Code, Qty, Reference — what actually
   arrived at the shop, signed for at the door.

- Match lines by SUPPLIER and ITEM CODE, normalized — trim it, ignore case, ignore spaces and
  dashes. A description typed in capitals is still the same part.
- Check every line's price against MY agreed price for that item, not against the bill's own
  arithmetic. A bill that agrees with itself can still be wrong about everything.
- Check every line's quantity against what the receiving log says arrived. Billing me for
  twenty-four when eighteen showed up is a finding, not a rounding difference.
- A credit memo is money owed to me, not an error. Keep it visible and subtract it.
- A price BELOW the agreed price is good news. Label it, don't flag it.

================================ PROMPT 3 ================================

Now make it trustworthy enough to send to a supplier with a straight face.

- All money in whole cents, one written-down rounding rule (round half up). No floating-point
  dollars anywhere, in any total.
- The billing period is typed into the page. The machine clock gets no vote: the same three
  files must produce the same audit on any computer, on any day. A line dated outside the
  period is listed, not judged.
- Receipts ACCUMULATE: twelve wipers delivered twice is twenty-four wipers, and a quantity
  check that only remembers the latest delivery slip is defamation. Consume the received
  balance line by line, in date order.
- A duplicate is the same GOODS at the same PRICES from the same supplier within a window of
  days — whatever the invoice number says. The same bill resubmitted under a new number is
  flagged whole, as one finding, and its lines are checked against nothing else.
- A small price difference inside a tolerance I set is labeled and left alone. I pick the
  tolerance; zero means zero.
- A line the page can't read — a date that isn't a date, a quantity that isn't a number, a
  printed line total that disagrees with its own quantity times price — is refused out loud
  with its row number and the reason. Never guessed at, never skipped in silence.
- The page audits itself on every run: bill lines in = clean + flagged + billed-twice +
  check-by-hand + credits + outside-the-period + refused; receiving rows in = counted +
  unmatched + refused; and the disputed total must re-add from its four causes AND from the
  per-supplier table, to the cent. If any audit fails, or too many rows were refused, print
  DO NOT TRUST THIS AUDIT across the top, say why, and withhold every dispute note — a wrong
  accusation is worse than a missed one.
- For each supplier with findings, draft a dispute note I can actually send: every disputed
  line named by invoice and date, every dollar traced, the credits acknowledged.

=========================== THE SECOND OPINION ===========================

The episode also used an independent checker — the same rules implemented again in Python by a
separate session, sharing no code with the page — run on the same three files. checker.py is in
this folder. That second opinion is what proved the page and the rules agree: 209 paired runs
over generated shops, 27,143 compared values, zero disagreements. When two implementations
disagree, one of them is wrong, and it is worth finding out which before you accuse a supplier.
